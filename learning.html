Subject & Object Pronouns

Who does it? Who receives it? 👀

💡 What Are Pronouns?

Pronouns are words we use instead of nouns.

Instead of repeating a name or thing, we can use a pronoun.

Milo is cute. Milo likes fish.

↓

Milo is cute. He likes fish.

👤 Subject Pronouns

Subject pronouns tell us who or what does the action.

SubjectExample
II like cats.
YouYou are kind.
HeHe plays football.
SheShe reads books.
ItIt is cute.
WeWe study English.
TheyThey are happy.
⭐ Remember! Subject + Verb

She likes music.
They play football.

🎯 Object Pronouns

Object pronouns tell us who or what receives the action.

SubjectObjectExample
ImeShe helps me.
YouyouI like you.
HehimI know him.
SheherWe help her.
ItitI feed it.
WeusThey invite us.
TheythemI see them.
⭐ Remember! Subject → does the action Object → receives the action

She helps me.
👤 does it → She
❤️ receives it → me

🔄 Let's Compare!

Rina likes Milo.

➡️

My friends help me.

➡️

I feed my cat.

➡️

🧠 Easy Trick

WHO DOES THE ACTION?→ SUBJECT
WHO RECEIVES THE ACTION?→ OBJECT

They love her.

Who loves? → They

Who receives the love? → her

Prepositions in English

Prepositions are words that tell us where something or someone is.

📖

Learning YO! · Writing

Descriptive Text

Let’s describe it! 🔎

💡 What Is a Descriptive Text?

A descriptive text describes a specific or particular person, animal, place, or thing. It tells us what someone or something is like.

👧 PERSON

My Best Friend, Rina

Rina is kind and friendly. She has long black hair.

🐱 ANIMAL

My Lovely Cat, Milo

Milo is small and cute. It has soft white fur.

🏫 PLACE

My School

My school is big and clean. It has a large garden.

📖 Read a Descriptive Text

Let’s see how a descriptive text works!

Milo, My Lovely Cat

Milo is my lovely cat. He is a domestic cat that lives with me and my family. He is my favorite pet because he is cute, friendly, and fun to be with.

Milo has soft white and orange fur. He has round eyes, a small pink nose, and a long tail. His body is small, but his legs are strong. He also wears a small bell around his neck.

Milo has some funny habits. He likes sleeping on the sofa in the afternoon. He also enjoys playing with a small ball and running around the house. Sometimes, he follows me from one room to another.

Milo is a friendly and playful cat. He is usually calm, but he can be very active when he plays. He is also quite curious and likes exploring new places. I love Milo because his cute and friendly personality always makes me happy.

↓🔎 Let’s see the structure↓

IDENTIFICATION

“Milo is my lovely cat.”

Introduces the subject.

DESCRIPTION

“Milo has soft white and orange fur. He has round eyes, a small pink nose, and a long tail.”

Tells us what the subject is like.

🧩 The Structure

1. IDENTIFICATION

Who or what are we describing?

“This is my lovely cat, Milo.”

2. DESCRIPTION

What is Milo like?

“Milo is small and cute.
It has soft white fur.”

✨ Describing Words

We often use adjectives to describe people, animals, places, and things.

beautiful cute friendly kind big small clean tall

👀 Look!

🐱

a cute cat

👧

a friendly girl

🏫

a big school

🧱 Language Features

Descriptive texts usually use:

Simple Present Tense

“Milo is cute.
It has soft fur.”

Adjectives

“Milo is small and cute.”

Remember

🔑 Remember!

A descriptive text describes a specific subject.

IDENTIFICATION + DESCRIPTION

“My Lovely Cat, Milo”
Milo is small and cute. It has soft white fur.

Narrative Text

Once upon a time... let’s tell a story! 📖

💡 Brief Explanation

A narrative text tells a series of fictional or imaginary events in chronological order. It usually opens by introducing the characters and setting, then a problem (a complication) arises that the characters must deal with, and it closes with a resolution. Common examples include fairy tales, fables, legends, and folk tales.

Purpose of the Text

🎯 Purpose of the Text

To amuse or entertain readers with a story that deals with a series of related problematic events which lead to a crisis or turning point, and finally find a resolution.

🧩 Generic Structure

1. ORIENTATION

Introduces characters, time, and place.

2. COMPLICATION

A problem or conflict arises.

3. RESOLUTION

The problem is solved.

4. CODA (optional)

A moral or closing message.

📖 Example

THE CLEVER MOUSE DEER

Once upon a time, a mouse deer lived happily near a river in the jungle. One day, a hungry crocodile blocked his path and wanted to eat him.

Suddenly, the mouse deer had an idea. “Wait,” he said calmly, “the king asked me to count how many crocodiles live in this river. Please call your friends to line up across the water.”

The crocodile believed him and gathered his friends in a row. The mouse deer then hopped from one crocodile’s back to another until he safely reached the other side.

In the end, the mouse deer escaped, and the crocodiles realized they had been tricked.

🧱 Tips / Language Focus

Simple Past Tense

lived, blocked, had, escaped — almost every verb is in the past form.

Time Connectives

once upon a time, suddenly, then, in the end, finally.

Specific Characters

a particular character (a mouse deer, a princess), not a general group.

Action Verbs

many action verbs are used to move the plot forward.

Recount Text

Let’s retell what really happened! 🗓️

💡 Brief Explanation

A recount text is a text that retells an event or experience that happened in the past, told in the order it happened (chronologically). Common examples include holiday recounts, activity reports, diary entries, and short biographies.

Purpose of the Text

🎯 Purpose of the Text

To retell events for the purpose of informing or entertaining the reader, by presenting events in the order in which they happened (chronological order).

🧩 Generic Structure

1. ORIENTATION

Who, when, and where the event took place.

2. EVENTS

A series of events in chronological order.

3. REORIENTATION

A closing remark or personal comment.

📖 Example

MY TRIP TO BOROBUDUR

Last month, my family and I visited Borobudur Temple in Magelang. We left home early in the morning so we could see the sunrise.

When we arrived, we climbed to the top level of the temple. The view of the misty hills was amazing. After that, we walked around and took a lot of photos near the stupas.

At noon, we had lunch at a small restaurant nearby before heading home.

Overall, it was a memorable trip, and I hope I can visit Borobudur again someday.

🧱 Tips / Language Focus

Simple Past Tense

visited, climbed, walked, had — used to tell events that already happened.

Time Connectives

last month, after that, at noon, finally, overall.

Personal Pronouns

often uses I, we, my family because it is a personal experience.

No Required Conflict

unlike narrative text, a problem is not required.

Procedure Text

Let’s follow the steps! 📋

💡 Brief Explanation

A procedure text contains the steps needed to make, operate, or do something. It is arranged in sequence so that readers can follow the instructions accurately and achieve the intended result. Common examples include recipes, instruction manuals, game rules, and tutorials.

Purpose of the Text

🎯 Purpose of the Text

To help readers do or make something completely, step by step, by describing how something is accomplished through a sequence of actions or steps.

🧩 Generic Structure

1. GOAL

The purpose — what will be made or done.

2. MATERIALS

The ingredients or tools needed (if any).

3. STEPS

The sequence of actions in order.

📖 Example

HOW TO MAKE A CUP OF TEA

Materials: a teabag, hot water, a cup, sugar (optional)
  1. First, boil some water until it is hot.
  2. Next, put a teabag into a cup.
  3. Then, pour the hot water into the cup.
  4. After that, let it steep for about three minutes.
  5. Add sugar if you like it sweet.
  6. Finally, stir well and your tea is ready to drink.

🧱 Tips / Language Focus

Imperative Sentences

commands without a subject: boil, put, pour, stir.

Sequence Connectives

first, next, then, after that, finally.

Action Verbs First

almost every step begins with an action verb.

Simple Present Tense

used for general instructions, not the past tense.

Report Text

Let’s explore the facts! 🔬

💡 Brief Explanation

A report text presents factual information about a whole class of things — such as a type of animal, plant, place, or phenomenon — based on observation and analysis. Unlike a descriptive text, which focuses on one particular thing, a report text describes general and permanent features shared by the whole group. Common examples include information reports on animals, plants, technology, and natural phenomena.

Purpose of the Text

🎯 Purpose of the Text

To present information about something as it is, generally, after observing and analyzing it systematically.

🧩 Generic Structure

1. GENERAL CLASSIFICATION

Introduces and classifies the subject — what it is or belongs to.

2. DESCRIPTION

Describes qualities, parts, behavior, or characteristics of the subject.

📖 Example

SEA TURTLES

Sea turtles are large marine reptiles that live in oceans around the world. They have existed for more than 100 million years.

Sea turtles have a hard shell that protects their body, and their front legs are shaped like flippers, which help them swim long distances. Unlike most reptiles, sea turtles cannot pull their head and legs into their shell.

Most sea turtles eat jellyfish, seaweed, and small fish. They usually live in warm, tropical waters, and female sea turtles return to the beach where they were born to lay their eggs.

🧱 Tips / Language Focus

Simple Present Tense

are, have, eat, live — describes general facts, not one-time events.

General Nouns

‘sea turtles’, not ‘a sea turtle’ or ‘my turtle’ — refers to the whole group.

No Personal Opinions

written objectively, without ‘I think’ or ‘I like’.

Relating & Action Verbs

be-verbs (are, have) for classification; action verbs (eat, swim) for behavior.

Learning YO! · Grammar

Simple Present Tense

Facts, habits, and routines! ☀️

💡 Brief Explanation

Simple present tense is a grammatical form used to talk about facts, habits, routines, and things that are generally true. It describes actions that happen regularly or situations that do not change over time.

Purpose / Function

🎯 Purpose / Function

To state facts, habits, routines, and general truths that are not limited to a specific time.

🧩 Formula

POSITIVE

S + V1 (+s/es for he/she/it)

NEGATIVE

S + do/does + not + V1

INTERROGATIVE

Do/Does + S + V1?

📖 Example

EVERYDAY ROUTINES

I wake up at six every morning. My sister brushes her teeth and gets dressed quickly.

We usually have breakfast together before school. My father drives us to school, but sometimes we walk.

Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius. The sun rises in the east and sets in the west.

🧱 Tips / Language Focus

Add -s / -es

for third person singular (he/she/it): goes, watches, studies.

Time Signals

always, usually, often, every day, on Mondays.

do / does

used for negatives and questions: I don’t like tea. Does she like tea?

Facts, Not ‘Now’

describes routines and general truths, not actions happening right now.

Simple Past Tense

Things that already happened! ⏳

💡 Brief Explanation

Simple past tense is used to talk about actions or situations that started and finished at a specific time in the past. It describes completed actions, not ongoing ones.

Purpose / Function

🎯 Purpose / Function

To talk about actions, events, or situations that happened and were completed in the past.

🧩 Formula

POSITIVE

S + V2 (regular: verb+ed; irregular: 2nd form)

NEGATIVE

S + did not (didn’t) + V1

INTERROGATIVE

Did + S + V1?

📖 Example

A DAY AT THE BEACH

Last Saturday, my friends and I went to the beach. We arrived early in the morning and set up our umbrella near the shore.

We swam in the sea and built a sandcastle together. Around noon, we ate lunch under the umbrella and rested for a while.

We didn’t leave until sunset because the view was so beautiful.

🧱 Tips / Language Focus

Regular Verbs

add -ed: played, walked, watched.

Irregular Verbs

change form: went, ate, saw, had.

Time Signals

yesterday, last week, … ago, in 2020.

Use did, Not doed

negatives/questions use did + base verb: didn’t go, Did you go?

Passive Voice

Focus on the action! 🔄

💡 Brief Explanation

Passive voice is a sentence structure used when the focus is on the action or the receiver of the action, rather than on who or what performs it. The subject of a passive sentence receives the action instead of doing it.

Purpose / Function

🎯 Purpose / Function

To emphasize the action or the object affected by it, especially when the doer is unknown, unimportant, or obvious.

🧩 Formula

SIMPLE PRESENT PASSIVE

S + am/is/are + V3 (+ by agent)

SIMPLE PAST PASSIVE

S + was/were + V3 (+ by agent)

AGENT (OPTIONAL)

‘by + doer’ — can be left out.

📖 Example: Active vs Passive

Let’s compare!

Workers built this bridge in 1990.

➡️

This bridge was built by workers in 1990.

The teacher checks our homework every day.

➡️

Our homework is checked by the teacher every day.

Someone stole my bike last night.

➡️

My bike was stolen last night.

🧱 Tips / Language Focus

Match ‘to be’ to the Tense

is/am/are for present, was/were for past.

Verb Becomes V3

the main verb changes to its past participle: written, built, checked, eaten.

‘by + agent’ Is Optional

can be dropped when unknown or unimportant: ‘The letter was sent.’

Common in Formal Writing

frequently used in reports, news articles, and academic writing.

Vocabulary: School Supplies

Learn everyday classroom words! ✏️

💡 Brief Explanation

This section introduces common school supplies vocabulary at A2 level. These are everyday objects students use in the classroom, useful for beginner to elementary learners building basic English vocabulary.

Learning Objective

🎯 Learning Objective

By the end of this section, you will be able to recognize, name, and use basic school supplies vocabulary in simple sentences.

🎒 School Supplies

Ten words every student should know.

✏️ pencil

I write with a pencil.

🖊️ pen

She has a blue pen.

❎ eraser

Can I borrow your eraser?

📏 ruler

Use a ruler to draw a straight line.

📓 notebook

I keep my notes in a notebook.

🎒 backpack

He carries his books in a backpack.

✂️ scissors

We use scissors to cut paper.

🧴 glue

Put some glue on the paper.

🖍️ crayon

The children colored with crayons.

🔧 sharpener

I sharpen my pencil with a sharpener.

🧱 Tips / Language Focus

Use ‘a’ Before Consonant Sounds

a pencil, a ruler, a backpack.

Use ‘an’ Before Vowel Sounds

an eraser.

Plural Forms

add -s for most words: pencils, erasers, notebooks.

‘Scissors’ Is Always Plural

we say ‘a pair of scissors’, not ‘a scissor’.
